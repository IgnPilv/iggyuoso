# Music theory and solfeggio curricula, zero to ~5 years (high-intermediate)

Scope: theory track for an adult self-taught piano learner (browser + MIDI). Structure and sequencing only; no copied text.

## ABRSM / Trinity / RCM: content per grade or level

### Takeaway
The three Anglo exam boards all move in the same order: notation and simple time, then a widening circle of keys (about 1 to 2 new sharps/flats per grade), interval number then quality, tonic triad then I/IV/V, then cadences and inversions (around Grade 4–5 / RCM Level 5–6), and only then harmony, figured bass and form (Grade 6+ / RCM Level 8–9). ABRSM/Trinity Grade 5, or RCM Level 6–8, is a fair "3–5 years in" theory target; Grade 6–7 content (cadences in four parts, sonata/rondo form, secondary sevenths, modulation) is the high-intermediate ceiling.

### Cited Findings
**ABRSM (topic list per grade, from mymusictheory.com navigation, which follows the ABRSM syllabus):**
- G1: major scales and scale degrees, key signatures (C, G, D, F majors in the real syllabus, not confirmed on the page), interval *numbers* only, simple time signatures, note/rest values, dotted and tied notes, beaming, tonic triads, dynamics/symbols/terms. — [MyMusicTheory](https://www.mymusictheory.com)
- G2: minor scales introduced, major and minor key signatures, intervals, adding bar lines and working out the time signature, rests, triplets, major/minor tonic triads. — [MyMusicTheory](https://www.mymusictheory.com)
- G3: more keys (E and A-flat major), melodic minor, interval *quality*, compound time signatures, grouping and beaming, rewriting rhythms, spotting errors. — [MyMusicTheory](https://www.mymusictheory.com)
- G4: keys up to 5 sharps/flats, chromatic scale, technical names of degrees (tonic, supertonic... leading note), double sharps/flats, enharmonics, any interval between two notes, 2/8 and 4/8, breves, double dots, duplets, triads and chords on degrees, ornaments, orchestral instruments. — [MyMusicTheory](https://www.mymusictheory.com)
- G5: keys up to 6 sharps/flats, scales in any clef, identifying the key, compound intervals, irregular time signatures and tuplets, chord II, chord inversions, cadences. — [MyMusicTheory](https://www.mymusictheory.com)
- ABRSM's companion book is "The AB Guide to Music Theory Grades 1–5". — [Millers Music](https://millersmusic.co.uk/products/abrsm-the-ab-guide-to-music-theory-grades-1-5)

### Inferences
- Common spine: (1) staff, clefs, note values, simple metre; (2) C/G/F major + A minor, tones/semitones; (3) interval numbers, then qualities; (4) tonic triad, then V, then IV; (5) compound metre; (6) more keys, one or two per level; (7) inversions + V7; (8) cadences (perfect/authentic first, then plagal, imperfect/half, interrupted); (9) ii, then all diatonic chords; (10) modulation to closely related keys; (11) form (binary, ternary, then rondo/sonata).
- Trinity and RCM introduce **chord symbols and Roman numerals together from the very first grade/level**; this suits a piano app where lead-sheet symbols are useful early.
- Trinity puts binary at G5, ternary at G6, rondo and sonata at G7. A "5 years to high-intermediate" app could hit sonata basics in year 4–5 as listening/analysis rather than writing.
- For an adult, the key-signature drip (about 2 keys per year) can be sped up, since it is mostly conceptual and can be drilled. The aural and keyboard side (playing in those keys) sets the real pace.

- I could not fetch the official ABRSM syllabus PDF; ABRSM per-grade key lists (e.g. G1 = C, G, D, F major) come from my own knowledge and are not confirmed here. The 2018+ ABRSM revision dropped some writing tasks (e.g. melody composition at G5); also not verified here.
- ABRSM Grade 6 overview not sourced (from training knowledge, it covers four-part harmony, figured bass and melody writing in a Baroque/Classical style).

## Soviet-tradition solfeggio (ДМШ) and Lithuania

### Takeaway
The Soviet/post-Soviet children's music school runs solfeggio once a week across 7 (or 8) years, all sung, dictated and aurally analysed, in parallel with instrument study. Its typical order: C major, stable/unstable degrees and tonic triad in year 1; a few keys and interval quality in year 2; by year 4–5 all diatonic intervals, tritones, primary triads with inversions, D7 and inversions, keys to 4 signs. Years 6–8 add VII7, II7, characteristic intervals, harmonic major, modulation and 7-sign keys. The standard textbook spine is Kalmykova & Fridkin (Калмыков–Фридкин) "Сольфеджио", plus Davydova, Fridkin's sight-reading book and others. I found no detailed Lithuanian programme.

### Cited Findings
- A St Petersburg 2nd-year working programme: keys G major, E minor, F major, D minor, D major; stable and unstable/leading (вводные) degrees; intervals prime to 7th, major/minor, perfect 4th/5th, with the year's goal being to build and sing up to the 4th; 2/4, 3/4, 4/4; dotted rhythm; sharp/flat/natural; oral, visual and melodic dictation; aural analysis of form, phrases, mode and type of minor. — [RP Solfedzhio 2nd year (SPb)](https://ddt-grad-tchudes.akrns.gov.spb.ru/images/doc/doki/23-24/RP/Karimova/RP_Solfedzhio_2go.pdf)
- Belarus Ministry of Culture 2023 solfeggio programme for gymnasium-colleges, grades V–IX (35 h/yr each), mapped to Kalmykov–Fridkin by school year:
  - V: keys to 4 signs; tritones in natural major and harmonic minor; "horn fifths" (золотой ход валторн); 6ths/7ths; interrupted cadence; dim triad and inversions; cadential 6/4; D7 inversions; four triad types; T, S, D with inversions; II, III, VI triads; 5–6 chord progressions by ear; dotted rhythms, 3/8, syncopation, triplets; 8-bar dictation (10 playings, 30 min). Textbooks: Kalmykov & Fridkin Part 1; Fridkin's sight-reading book; Davydova "Сольфеджио для ДМШ, 4 класс". — [ADU.by solfeggio programme](https://www.adu.by/images/2024/02/muz.-instrum._iskustv_V-IX_solfedgio.pdf)
  - VI: keys to 5 signs; **harmonic major**; characteristic intervals; augmented triad; VII7 (diminished and half-diminished) with resolution; triads of all degrees; accompaniment figuration types; 6/8, dotted quarter + 2 sixteenths. — [ADU.by](https://www.adu.by/images/2024/02/muz.-instrum._iskustv_V-IX_solfedgio.pdf)
  - VII: keys to 6 signs; circle of fifths; passing and neighbour notes (diatonic and chromatic); II7 and inversions; sevenths on all degrees; 3/2, 6/4, 9/8, 12/8; dictation with chromatic passing tones. — [ADU.by](https://www.adu.by/images/2024/02/muz.-instrum._iskustv_V-IX_solfedgio.pdf)
  - VIII: 7 signs; melodic major; chromatic scale; modulation to closely related keys (first-degree kinship); seven types of seventh chord; enharmonic dim7; mixed metres; 32nds. — [ADU.by](https://www.adu.by/images/2024/02/muz.-instrum._iskustv_V-IX_solfedgio.pdf)
  - IX: pentatonic, diatonic modes, double-harmonic, whole-tone and octatonic scales; chromatic intervals; compound intervals; modulating progressions. — [ADU.by](https://www.adu.by/images/2024/02/muz.-instrum._iskustv_V-IX_solfedgio.pdf)
- Many Russian regional schools publish 7-year and 8-year "Сольфеджио" programmes (e.g. Tatarstan, Vologda) as ДООП documents. They exist, but I could not fetch them. — [edu.tatar.ru (7-year)](https://edu.tatar.ru/upload/storage/org5919/files/сольфеджио%20фоно%207%20лет.doc); [Vologda ДООП 7 лет](https://d22305.edu35.ru/images/ДООП_Сольфеджио_7_лет.pdf); a Baeva–Zebryak 1–2 class textbook is listed by a school — [edu-1.tatar.ru](https://edu-1.tatar.ru/upload/storage/org5611/files/Баева%20Зебряк%20Сольфеджио%201-2%20класс.pdf)
- Lithuania: the national encyclopedia defines solfeggio (solfedžio) as a system for training musical hearing. Practice is singing with solmization syllables while beating time; singing scales, intervals, chords and inversions; writing and singing back dictated melodies and chords. It credits Jaques-Dalcroze, Kodály, Ostrovsky, Sposobin and the Lithuanian V. Venckus as methodological influences. — [VLE: solfedžio](https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/solfedzio/)
- Lithuanian arts gymnasium (Šiauliai S. Sondeckis) publishes a music-theory methodology document, but its content was not readable. — [sondeckis.lt](https://sondeckis.lt/lt/dokumentubankas/preview/1091)

### Inferences
- ДМШ year ≈ Belarus grade minus 1 (Belarus grade V uses Kalmykov–Fridkin's 4th-class section). So: ДМШ 4th class ≈ keys to 4 signs, D7 + inversions, cadential 6/4, tritones; ДМШ 5th ≈ harmonic major, VII7, triads of all degrees; ДМШ 6–7th ≈ II7, all sevenths, modulation, circle of fifths complete.
- ДМШ is far more aural than the Anglo boards. Every concept is also sung, heard in aural analysis ("слуховой анализ") and written from dictation. Its signature drills (interval/chord ID out of key, chord-progression ID by ear, 8-bar melodic dictation after a fixed number of playings) translate directly to app drills with sound.
- Signature Soviet concepts the Anglo boards lack, worth adopting: stable vs unstable degrees and their resolution; tritones and characteristic intervals (aug2/dim7 in harmonic modes) resolving in key; harmonic major; "four triad types" drill; chord chains of the T–S–D type sung/played as a sequence.
- Lithuanian music schools almost certainly follow a similar Soviet-derived 7–8-year model (shared history; VLE names Ostrovsky and Sposobin). Not confirmed by any source found.

### Gaps
- Year-by-year content for ДМШ classes 1, 3 and 4 is not sourced: the edu.tatar.ru documents failed to fetch. From training knowledge (unverified): class 1 = C, G, F, D major, tonic triad, stable degrees, 2/4–3/4, quarter/eighth/half notes; class 3 = minor (three forms), keys to 3 signs, primary triads, intervals by quality, dotted rhythms, sixteenths; class 4 = keys to 4 signs, D7, triad inversions, syncopation.
- Baraboshkina (pre-school/1st-class workbooks) and Davydova year content not confirmed beyond Davydova being a standard text.
- Lithuanian solfeggio programme: no source found. Whether schools use relative solmization (the Lithuanian tradition is often linked with relative "jo-le-mi" syllables) or fixed do is unverified. Searches on Venckus/relative solmization produced nothing.

## Kodály, Dalcroze, Orff, Gordon: order and principles

### Takeaway
Child-centred methods agree on **sound before symbol**: experience a sound or rhythm by singing, moving or playing, then name it, then read and write it. Kodály orders pitch as sol–mi → la → do → re (do-pentatonic) → low la/sol → fa and ti (diatonic) → minor/modes. Rhythm goes beat → ta / ti-ti → rest → half note → sixteenths → dotted/syncopation → compound metre. Gordon formalises this as a skill-learning sequence: aural/oral → verbal association → symbolic association → composite synthesis → inference (generalisation, improvisation, theory).

### Cited Findings
- Kodály sequence (Wichita State Kodály programme): K: steady beat, word rhythm, high/low, s–m. G1: ta, ti-ti, quarter rest, 2-metre, bar lines, s–m, then l. G2: half note, 4-metre, four sixteenths (ti-ri-ti-ri), conducting in 2/3/4, do, re, do-pentatonic. G3: dotted half, 3-metre, ti-ti-ri patterns, syncopation; absolute letter names in C, G, F; extended pentatonic (low la, sol, high do); canons. G4: whole note, eighth rest, tam-ti/ti-tam (dotted quarter-eighth); la- and sol-pentatonic; fa, whole and half steps, pentachord, hexachord, B-flat; modes; hearing I and V, triads. G5: mixed metre, anacrusis, 6/8, re-pentatonic, ti, major and minor scales, two-part singing. — [Wichita State Kodály sequence](https://wichita.edu/academics/fine_arts/music/kodaly/documents/G_2009_Wichita_A_KODALY_SEQUENCE_FINAL.pdf)
- Gordon's Music Learning Theory: "audiation" (coined 1975) means hearing and understanding music not physically present, "audiation is to music what thought is to language." Discrimination learning runs aural/oral (imitate patterns on neutral syllables) → verbal association (solfa/rhythm syllables, names like tonic/dominant) → partial synthesis → symbolic association (notation linked to known patterns) → composite synthesis (read/write patterns, identify tonality and metre). Inference learning covers generalisation, creativity/improvisation, and theoretical understanding (letter names, intervals, key signatures, cadences). — [Wikipedia: Gordon music learning theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_music_learning_theory)
- Gordon's six stages of audiation run from momentary retention through anticipating patterns. — [Wikipedia: Gordon music learning theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_music_learning_theory)

### Inferences
- For the app: every theory node should run **hear → name → see → read/play → create**. Example: hear major vs minor triads, then learn the names, then see them on the staff, then play them on MIDI, then improvise with them.
- Kodály's pentatonic-first order (no semitones, so no "wrong" clashes) suits improvisation on black keys for a complete beginner. Its rhythm order (quarter/eighth before half/whole) differs from piano method books, which often start with whole/half notes. Either works, but the app should pick one and stay consistent.
- Gordon's insistence on teaching **tonic/dominant function by ear before notation** suggests functional ear drills (hear I vs V, cadence endings) from year 1, long before written cadence theory in year 3–4.
- Relative solfa (movable do, la-based minor) helps with function and transposition. Letter names are needed for keyboard work, so the app can use both: degrees/solfa for hearing, letters for playing.

### Gaps
- Dalcroze eurhythmics (movement-based rhythm, solfège-rythmique, improvisation) and Orff Schulwerk (speech → body percussion → barred instruments, ostinato and bordun, pentatonic improvisation) were not sourced in this pass. The outline above is training knowledge and should be checked before it is cited.
- Gordon's specific pattern order for tonalities (major and harmonic minor first, then modes) and metres (usual duple and triple first) was not on the page fetched.

## Interactive (with sound) vs conceptual items; example drills

### Takeaway
Ear-training sites and ДМШ practice show what benefits from sound: interval ID, chord-quality ID, scale/mode ID, cadence and progression ID, rhythmic and melodic dictation, and sight-singing/playing back. Spelling-type knowledge (key signatures, naming intervals and chords on the staff, terms, form labels) works as visual/conceptual drills, though sound feedback still helps.

### Cited Findings
- teoria.com splits its exercises into three categories (reading, writing, ear training, from training knowledge, since the category names did not render) and offers predefined levels. — [teoria.com exercises help](https://www.teoria.com/en/help/exercises-help.php?sec=nc)
- ToneDear and similar sites offer interval, chord, scale and progression ear training as separate drills. — [ToneDear](https://tonedear.com/); comparison of ear-training apps — [Hooktheory blog](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/ear-training-apps-and-exercises/)
- University musicianship guides group practice as sight-singing, dictation (melodic, harmonic, rhythmic) and identification of intervals/chords/seventh chords. — [McGill musicianship guide](https://libraryguides.mcgill.ca/musicianship); [LibreTexts: Equipping the Musical Ear, seventh-chords practice](https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/Sierra_College/Equipping_the_Musical_Ear/10%3A_Seventh_Chords/10.07%3A_Additional_Practice)
- ДМШ formal drills: aural analysis of intervals, triads and D7 out of key; progressions of 5–10 chords in a key; 8-bar melodic dictation within 30 min / 10 playings. — [ADU.by](https://www.adu.by/images/2024/02/muz.-instrum._iskustv_V-IX_solfedgio.pdf)

### Inferences
Mapping for the app (MIDI keyboard as the answer device):
- **Sound essential**: pitch matching (hear a note, find it on the keyboard); interval ID and play-back; major/minor/dim/aug triad quality; triad inversion by ear; V7 vs major triad; cadence type (perfect/plagal/imperfect/interrupted); progression ID (I–IV–V–I, I–vi–IV–V, ii–V–I); scale/mode ID; rhythmic dictation (tap it back); melodic dictation (play it back); stable vs unstable degree and resolution; functional hearing ("which degree is this?" after a key is set).
- **Sound helps** (play it to check): building intervals, chords and scales on the keyboard from a name; transposition of a short melody; chord symbols → voicing; harmonising a melody with I/IV/V; improvising over a progression.
- **Conceptual / visual**: note reading (staff ↔ key), key signatures and the circle of fifths, enharmonics, time signatures and beaming, terms and expression marks, form labelling (binary/ternary/rondo/sonata, with listening examples to make it concrete), Roman-numeral analysis on the page.
- Adaptive difficulty as in teoria/ToneDear: let the user choose the set (e.g. only m3/M3/P5) and grow it as accuracy rises. That maps directly onto a mastery tree.

### Gaps
- No pedagogical study found here on how well app-based ear training transfers to playing; nothing on recommended interval-introduction order beyond common practice (often P8/P5/M3 first, or Kodály's m3 first via sol–mi).

## Concept dependencies (prerequisite graph)

### Takeaway
A defensible graph can be built from the shared ordering in ABRSM/Trinity/RCM and ДМШ. Key bottlenecks: tones/semitones → major scale → key signatures → intervals by quality → triads → inversions/V7 → cadences → harmonic function → harmonisation/modulation → form.

### Cited Findings
- Tones/semitones and degrees come before scales and key signatures (Trinity G1; RCM L1). Interval number comes before quality (ABRSM G1 vs G3; RCM L2 vs L3–4). Tonic triad comes before V and IV (Trinity G1→G3→G4; RCM Prep→L3→L4). Inversions come before cadences (Trinity G2–4 → cadences G3–5; RCM L5 → L6). V7 (G4/L5) comes before diminished sevenths and secondary sevenths (G6–7/L7). Modulation (G5) comes before sonata form (G7). — [Trinity syllabus PDF](https://www.newschool.ie/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Theory-of-Music-Syllabus-2009.pdf); [RCM 2016 Highlights](https://admin.rcmusic.com/sites/default/files/files/RCM-Theory-Syllabus-2016-Highlights.pdf); [MyMusicTheory](https://www.mymusictheory.com)
- In ДМШ: tritones, D7 inversions and the cadential 6/4 (grade V) come before VII7 and harmonic major (VI), which come before II7 and all sevenths (VII), which come before modulation (VIII). — [ADU.by](https://www.adu.by/images/2024/02/muz.-instrum._iskustv_V-IX_solfedgio.pdf)
- Kodály/Gordon: aural experience and syllables come before notation and theory names. — [Wichita](https://wichita.edu/academics/fine_arts/music/kodaly/documents/G_2009_Wichita_A_KODALY_SEQUENCE_FINAL.pdf); [Wikipedia: Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_music_learning_theory)

### Inferences
Proposed graph (A → B means A is a prerequisite for B), with a rough year for the adult track:

**Year 1 (≈ ABRSM/Trinity G1–2, RCM Prep–L2, ДМШ 1–2)**
- Keyboard geography → letter names → staff (treble) → bass clef → ledger lines
- Pulse → note values (q, h, w, e) → simple metre 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 → rests → ties/dots → anacrusis
- Semitone/tone → major scale (C, G, F) → key signature (1 sharp/flat) → scale degrees and stable/unstable degrees
- Interval number (2nd–8ve), heard and played → tonic triad (C, G, F) → chord symbol C / Roman numeral I
- Major vs minor sound (ear) → relative minor (A, E, D), natural then harmonic
- Dynamics, tempo, articulation terms (with expression in playing)
- Ear: high/low, pitch matching, same/different, major/minor triad, step vs skip

**Year 2 (≈ G2–3, RCM L3–4, ДМШ 3)**
- Interval quality (M/m 2nd, 3rd; P4, P5, P8; then 6ths, 7ths) → triad qualities (M, m)
- Keys to 2–3 signs; melodic minor; circle of fifths (part)
- Compound metre 6/8; dotted rhythms; sixteenths; triplets; syncopation
- Primary triads I, IV, V → simple harmonisation of melodies with I/V, then I/IV/V → lead-sheet symbols (C, F, G7, Am)
- Inversions of triads (heard and played) → perfect/authentic cadence by ear and on paper
- Transposition of 5-finger melodies (by interval and by degree)
- Improvisation: pentatonic, question/answer phrases
- Form: phrases, same/different, AB / ABA by ear

**Year 3 (≈ G4, RCM L5–6, ДМШ 4)**
- Keys to 4–5 signs; technical degree names; enharmonics; chromatic scale; double accidentals
- All diatonic intervals, aug4/dim5 (tritone) and resolution
- V7 + inversions + resolution; dim triad (vii°)
- Cadences: perfect, plagal, imperfect/half; cadential 6/4
- Function: T–S–D groups, harmonic rhythm
- Passing and neighbour notes
- Binary form; simple 8-bar melody writing; lead-sheet playing with I–IV–V–vi
- Ear: cadence ID, I–IV–V progressions, chord inversion ID, melodic dictation (4 bars)

**Year 4 (≈ G5, RCM L7–8, ДМШ 5–6)**
- All keys; full circle of fifths; modes; pentatonic, blues, whole-tone scales
- Compound intervals, interval inversion
- ii, iii, vi chords; interrupted cadence; ii–V–I; dominant and diminished seventh types; harmonic major (optional)
- Modulation to dominant or relative (by ear and on paper)
- Ternary form; theme and variations; harmonising a melody with full diatonic set; Alberti and other accompaniment patterns; improvising over I–vi–IV–V and 12-bar blues
- Ear: seventh-chord quality, progression dictation, 8-bar melodic dictation

**Year 5 (≈ G6–7, RCM L8–9, ДМШ 6–7)**
- All seventh chords on all degrees; II7; secondary dominants (V/V); suspensions; pivot-chord modulation; tierce de Picardie; Phrygian cadence
- Figured bass basics / four-part voice-leading basics (parallels)
- Forms: rondo, sonatina/sonata basics (exposition–development–recap), minuet/scherzo and trio
- Composition: 8–16-bar melody with harmonisation; improvisation with secondary dominants; lead-sheet voicings (sevenths, slash chords)
- Ear: modulation ID, secondary dominants, longer progressions

Hard edges in the graph:
- interval quality → triad quality → inversions → V7 → cadences → function → harmonisation → modulation → sonata form
- semitone → major scale → key signatures → minor forms → circle of fifths → transposition/modulation
- pulse → simple metre → dotted/syncopation → compound metre → mixed/irregular metres
- ear: major/minor triad → cadence by ear → progression by ear → modulation by ear
