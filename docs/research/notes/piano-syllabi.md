# Graded piano performance syllabi and method books (beginner to grade 5–6) for a 5-year curriculum

Scope note: primary sources fetched were the ABRSM 2025 & 2026 and 2027 & 2028 piano qualification specifications, an RCM 2022 technical-requirements chart (teacher-made from the official syllabus) plus the official RCM 2022 Piano Syllabus PDF (first ~78k characters only), a full 8-year Russian children's music school (DMSh) piano programme from the St Petersburg Rachmaninov school, a Russian programme framework (hours), Alfred's Adult All-in-One Level 1 contents, and the Faber Adult Piano Adventures publisher page. Trinity, the Lithuanian programmes, and the Faber/Bastien/Thompson/Suzuki tables of contents came back thin. See the Gaps sections.

## 1. ABRSM piano grades Initial–6: technical work, sight-reading, aural, time

### Takeaway
ABRSM is the most explicit about cumulative sight-reading parameters and gives scale/arpeggio tempo floors for every grade (crotchet 54 at Initial up to minim 72 at Grade 6). Hands-together, 2-octave major and minor scales arrive at Grade 2–3. Four-octave scales and dominant/diminished 7ths arrive at Grade 6. The requirements stay unchanged in the 2027 & 2028 syllabus; only the pieces change.

### Cited Findings
**Status and structure**
- The 2025 & 2026 syllabus is current in 2026. The 2027 & 2028 specification (valid 1 Jan 2027 – 31 Dec 2028) says "The Scales and arpeggios, Sight-reading and Aural test requirements stay the same as the preceding syllabus." Only the repertoire lists change, and 2025–26 pieces may be played until 31 Dec 2027. — [ABRSM 2027 & 2028 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/Piano%20Practical%20Grades%20Qualification%20Specification%202027%20&%202028.pdf)
- An exam consists of 3 pieces (one each from Lists A, B, C), scales & arpeggios, sight-reading and aural tests. List A is "generally faster moving and require technical agility". List B is "more lyrical and invite expressive playing". List C covers "a wide variety of musical traditions, styles and characters". At Initial–Grade 3 one piece may be a duet. — [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf)
- All scales and arpeggios are played from memory, legato and in even notes, without pedalling. — [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf)
- To enter Grade 6 you must already have passed ABRSM Grade 5 Music Theory, Grade 5 Practical Musicianship, or a Grade 5 solo Jazz instrument. This rule applies to Grades 6–8. — [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf); [ABRSM 2027 & 2028 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/Piano%20Practical%20Grades%20Qualification%20Specification%202027%20&%202028.pdf)

**Scales and arpeggios by grade** (HS = hands separately, HT = hands together) — all from [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf)
- **Initial:** C major and D minor scales (D minor in natural, harmonic or melodic form, candidate's choice), 1 octave HS. C major contrary motion over a 5-finger span (a 5th) HT. C major and D minor arpeggios over a 5th, HS.
- **Grade 1:** C major 1 octave HT. G and F majors and A and D minors, HS (the extraction said 2 octaves; the 2027 summary says "1 or 2 octaves"). C major contrary motion 1 octave. G major and A minor arpeggios 1 octave HS.
- **Grade 2:** G and F majors and A and D minors, 2 octaves HT. D and A majors and E and G minors, 2 octaves HS. C major contrary motion 2 octaves. Chromatic scale from D, 1 octave HS. Arpeggios in D and A majors and E and G minors, 2 octaves HS.
- **Grade 3:** D and A majors and E and G minors, 2 octaves HT. B♭ and E♭ majors and B and C minors, 2 octaves HS. E major contrary motion 2 octaves. Chromatic contrary motion from D, 1 octave. Arpeggios: D, A, E minor and G minor HT; B♭, E♭, B minor and C minor HS, all 2 octaves.
- **Grade 4:** B♭ and E♭ majors and B and C minors, 2 octaves HT. B, F♯ and A♭ majors and F♯ and F minors, 2 octaves HS. Contrary motion in E♭ major and C harmonic minor. Chromatic from F♯, 2 octaves HT similar motion. Arpeggios in the same split (first group HT, second HS).
- **Grade 5:** A, E, B, F♯ and D♭ majors and F♯, C♯, G♯, E♭ and B♭ minors (harmonic or melodic), 2 octaves HT legato. Staccato scales in A♭ major and F minor, HS. Contrary motion in D♭ major and C♯ harmonic minor. Chromatic contrary motion with the hands a major 3rd apart (LH F♯, RH A♯). Arpeggios: A, E, B, F♯, A♭ and D♭ majors and F♯, C♯, G♯, E♭, F and B♭ minors, 2 octaves HT. Diminished 7th from B, 2 octaves HS.
- **Grade 6:** D, F, A♭ and B majors and D, F, G♯ and B minors (harmonic and melodic), **4 octaves HT**, legato or staccato at the examiner's choice. Contrary motion in the same keys (harmonic minor), 2 octaves. Chromatic scale 4 octaves HT. Arpeggios 4 octaves HT, root position. Dominant 7ths in D, F, A♭ and B resolving on the tonic, 4 octaves HT. Diminished 7th from G♯, 4 octaves HT.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scales (incl. contrary motion, chromatic) | q=54 | q=60 | q=66 | q=80 | q=100 | h=60 | h=72 | h=80 | h=88 |
| Arpeggios (incl. dominant/diminished 7ths) | q=52 | q=58 | q=63 | q=72 | q=80 | h=44 | h=50 | h=56 | h=66 |

— [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf). The footnote says "All speeds relate to the rhythmic grouping [symbol]", but the symbol did not render, so the note value per beat could not be confirmed (see Gaps).

**Sight-reading parameters (cumulative)** — [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf)
- **Initial:** 4 bars in 4/4 (the table also shows a 6-bar 2/4 item). C major and D minor. Hands separately in 5-finger position (tonic to dominant). Legato phrases, staccato, f and p.
- **G1:** adds 3/4; G and F majors and A minor; any 5-finger position; occasional accidentals (minor keys only); dotted notes; slurs; accents; mf and mp; cresc./dim. hairpins.
- **G2:** adds D major and E and G minors; **hands together**; whole-note and dotted-crotchet patterns; ties; pp.
- **G3:** up to 8 bars; adds 3/8; A, B♭ and E♭ majors and B minor; **outside 5-finger position**; 2-note chords in either hand; simple semiquaver patterns.
- **G4:** about 8 bars; adds 6/8; anacrusis; chromatic notes; pause signs; tenuto.
- **G5:** about 8–12 bars; adds E and A♭ majors and F♯ and C minors; 4-part chords (no more than 2 notes in either hand); simple syncopation; rit. at the end; ff.
- **G6:** about 12–16 bars; adds 9/8, 5/8 and 5/4; C♯ and F minors; triplets; clef changes; **right (sustain) pedal**.

**Time per grade (official estimates)** — [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf)

|---|---|---|

These are described as average preparation-time estimates that vary by individual, and they count only time spent preparing for that grade.

### Inferences
- Summing total qualification time gives Initial to G5 ≈ 640 h and Initial to G6 ≈ 860 h. At 30 min/day, 5 days a week (~130 h/yr), that is ~5 years to G5 and ~6.6 years to G6. At 40 min/day every day (~240 h/yr), it is ~2.7 years to G5 and ~3.6 years to G6. ABRSM's hours are a floor for a "grade-only" student; most teachers add repertoire beyond the exam. 5 years at 20–40 min/day therefore lands an adult around **G5, possibly G6**.
- Useful benchmark ladder for an app:
  - Initial: 5-finger, HS.
  - G1: 1 octave, first HT scale.
  - G2: 2-octave HT scales and the first chromatic scale.
  - G3: hands leave 5-finger position in sight-reading; semiquavers.
  - G4: 6/8, anacrusis, q=100 scales.
  - G5: all keys up to 5 sharps/flats; syncopation; diminished 7th.
  - G6: 4-octave HT scales; dominant 7ths; pedal in sight-reading; triplets.
- The sight-reading feature list maps directly onto unlockable "reading skills" in a mastery tree: key, time signature, hand position, note values, dynamics and articulation symbols.
- The "from memory, even, legato, no pedal" rule is a ready scoring rubric for scale drills (evenness of IOI, legato overlap, tempo ≥ target).

### Gaps
- The rhythmic grouping in the speed footnote did not render. ABRSM convention is believed to be quavers (2 notes per beat) at the crotchet speeds and semiquavers (4 notes per minim, i.e. 8 per bar of 4/4?) at the minim speeds. This was not confirmed from the text and should be checked against the PDF visually.
- Aural test content (pages 45–52 of the spec) and the mark scheme (page 57) were not in the extracted text. From general knowledge, and **not verified here**: the standard ABRSM marks are pieces 30+30+30, scales 21, sight-reading 21, aural 18, total 150, with pass 100, merit 120 and distinction 130. Aural at the low grades covers clapping the pulse, echo singing, and identifying features such as dynamics and articulation; cadences and modulation come later.

## 2. RCM (Royal Conservatory, Canada) Preparatory A – Level 7, and Trinity

### Takeaway
RCM's technical tables are the most granular. Each level lists keys, HS/HT, number of octaves and metronome markings. RCM keeps scales slower than ABRSM at the same nominal level but adds triads with inversions very early (Prep A) and 4-note chords and full 7th-chord families by Level 6–7. Trinity data could not be retrieved in detail.

### Cited Findings
**RCM technical requirements, 2022 Piano Syllabus** — from the [RCM 2022 Technical Requirements Charts (teacher-made from the official syllabus)](https://colorinmypiano.com/wp-content/files/RCM_2022_Technical_Requirements_Charts_Prep-10.pdf). The note-value symbols were garbled in extraction ("iq" probably means eighth notes; "jjjq" probably sixteenth notes).
- **Prep A** (C, G, D, A minor): legato and staccato pentascales HS ending with a blocked triad, q=100 (in quarters). Broken triad sequence HS, 1 octave, ascending stepwise on white keys, in triplets at q=60. Blocked triad sequence HS at q=72.
- **Prep B** (C, G, D, A, F; A, E and D minor): legato and staccato pentascales HS, eighths at q=60. 1-octave natural minor scales HS at q=60. Contrary-motion scale HT, 1 octave, at q=60. Tonic triads, broken, root position and inversions, HS, 1 octave, in triplets at q=50.
- **Level 1** (C, G, F; A, E, D minor): 2-octave scales HS (minor natural and harmonic) at q=69 in eighths. Contrary motion HT, 2 octaves. Chromatic 1 octave HS. Triads with inversions: broken in triplets at q=50; blocked at q=100.
- **Level 2** (C, G, F, B♭; E, D, G minor): 2-octave scales HS (minor harmonic and melodic) at q=80. Formula pattern HT, 2 octaves. Chromatic 1 octave HS. Triads: broken at q=60; blocked at q=112.
- **Level 3** (D, F, B♭; B, D, G minor): 2-octave scales **HT** at q=80. Formula pattern. Chromatic 1 octave HS. Triads with inversions, 2 octaves HS: broken at q=69; blocked at q=120.
- **Level 4** (D, A, B♭, E♭; B, G, C minor): scales HT, 2 octaves, at q=92. Formula pattern (harmonic minor). Chromatic HS at q=104. Triads HT, 2 octaves: broken at q=60; blocked at q=120. **First arpeggios:** tonic, root position, HS, 2 octaves, at q=72 in eighths.
- **Level 5** (A, E, F, A♭; A, E, F minor): scales HT at q=104 in eighths. Formula pattern. Chromatic **HT** 1 octave at q=104. Tonic triads with inversions HT, 2 octaves, ending with a **I–V–I cadence**. Dominant 7th with inversions HS (broken at q=72; blocked at q=60). Arpeggios HS, 2 octaves, at q=80.
- **Level 6** (G, E, B, D♭; G, E, B, C♯ minor): scales HT, 2 octaves, **in sixteenths at q=60**. Formula pattern. Chromatic HT, 2 octaves. Triads HT, broken at q=80. Dominant 7th and leading-tone diminished 7th with inversions HS, 2 octaves. Arpeggios (tonic, dominant 7th, diminished 7th) HS, 2 octaves, in eighths at q=92.
- **Level 7** (C, D, F, A♭, G♭; C, D, F, G♯, F♯ minor): scales HT, 2 octaves, in sixteenths at q=76. Formula pattern (major and harmonic minor). Chromatic HT. Tonic 4-note chords broken HT with a I–IV–V–I cadence. Dominant 7th and diminished 7th HT (broken in sixteenths at q=60; blocked at q=80). Arpeggios **HT**, 2 octaves, in sixteenths at q=60 (tonic with inversions; dominant 7th and diminished 7th root position).

**RCM musicianship, Prep A – Level 3** — [RCM Piano Syllabus 2022](https://teacherportal.rcmusic.com/getattachment/57f3734d-97e5-4777-b67e-4b1111ee31a3/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf)
- **Prep A:** 3 pieces, no etudes.
  - Ear: clapback of about 2 bars; identify major or minor root-position triads; playback of 4 notes from the tonic or mediant in C or G.
  - Sight-reading: a rhythm of about 2 bars; two 4-note stepwise melodies, one per hand, with only the first fingering given.
- **Prep B:** same as Prep A, except the playback may be in A minor and the sight-reading melody is divided between the hands on the grand staff.
- **Level 1:** 3 pieces (Lists A/B/C) plus 1 etude.
  - Ear: intervals m3 and M3 ascending and descending; clapback of 2–3 bars; playback of 5 notes.
  - Sight-reading: a 4-bar melody divided between the hands in C, G or F major or A minor.
- **Level 2:** 3 pieces plus 1 etude.
  - Ear: adds the P5 interval; chords played blocked only.
  - Sight-reading: a 4-bar rhythm with rests; a 4-bar melody that may go beyond 5-finger position; keys add D minor.
- **Level 3:** 3 pieces plus 2 contrasting etudes.
  - Ear: intervals m3, M3, P4 and P5; identify the root, 3rd or 5th of a broken triad; playback of 5–6 notes.
  - Sight-reading: a 4-bar passage **hands together** in C, G, D or F major or A or D minor.
- Theory co-requisites apply from Level 5 up; Level 4 has none.
- Level 3 marking: repertoire 56 (A16, B18, C16, memory 6), technical 24 (tests 12, etudes 12), ear 10, sight-reading 10, out of 100. Pass is 60.

**Trinity**
- From 2023, each grade has a 12-piece exam book plus an optional "Extended Edition" with 21 pieces (Baroque to present) and scales/arpeggios exercises. Candidates choose 3 pieces and may substitute their own composition. Exams are available in person or digitally. — [Interlude.hk on Trinity's 2023 piano syllabus](https://interlude.hk/introducing-trinity-college-londons-new-piano-syllabus/)

### Inferences
- RCM gives the app a ready "metronome ladder" for scales: q=60–69 in eighths (Prep B/L1), q=80 (L2–3), q=92–104 (L4–5), then sixteenths at q=60–76 (L6–7) ≈ eighths at q=120–152. ABRSM's Grade 4 q=100 sits between RCM L4 and L5.
- RCM introduces **triads and inversions before arpeggios** (inversions from Prep B, arpeggios at Level 4). It also puts 7th chords at L5–7 and cadences at L5. This gives a harmony-forward technical spine that fits well with theory lessons.
- Ear-training progression for the pitch-training feature, from RCM: major/minor triad quality (Prep) → m3/M3 (L1) → +P5 (L2) → +P4 and chord-member identification (L3). Playback length grows from 4 notes (Prep) to 5–6 (L3). Clapback grows from 2 to 4 bars.

### Gaps
- The RCM ear-test and sight-reading details for Levels 4–7 were not in the portion of the syllabus PDF that could be extracted (the text cut off at the Level 3 repertoire).
- The exact RCM note values ("iq", "jjjq") need checking against the official syllabus.
- Trinity's current scale and arpeggio lists, tempi, and supporting tests (sight-reading, aural, improvisation, musical knowledge) were not retrieved. The article above pointed to trinitycollege.com/qualifications/music/grade-exams/piano.

## 3. Eastern European / Soviet-tradition music school (DMSh / DShI; Lithuania)

### Takeaway
The Russian 8-year pre-professional programme is the most demanding model. It sets 14–18 pieces a year from year 2, in a fixed genre mix (etudes, polyphony, large form, pieces). Year 1 starts with single-line pieces using non legato, then legato. Staccato and pedal arrive in year 2, and 4-octave scales in year 3. A technical exam each spring is taken from year 3, and sight-reading is set about 2–3 classes below the current class.

### Cited Findings
All findings below come from the [Rachmaninov school, St Petersburg, 8-year programme "Specialty and sight-reading" (2020)](https://rahmaninovschool.spb.ru/f/dpop_2020_8_fortepiano_spec_i_chtenie.pdf) unless another source is named.
- **Year 1:** goals are getting to know the instrument, elementary playing skills on single-line pieces, ear, memory, rhythm, music literacy, elementary sight-reading, and **non legato and legato touches**.
  - First semester: 10–15 single-line pieces.
  - Second semester: 8–10 two-hand pieces, including 3–4 etudes.
  - End-of-year exam: 3–4 contrasting two-hand pieces.
  - Technical: 3 scales (C major, A minor and a third major, which the extraction gave as "B major" and is probably G) over 2 octaves with both hands. Contrary-motion scales from one note, 2 octaves. Short and long arpeggios HS, 2 octaves. Chromatic scale HS, 2 octaves. Triads with inversions HS, 1 octave.
  - The introduction more modestly recommends 2–3 major scales HS in parallel and contrary motion. The document is internally inconsistent here.
- **Year 2:** confident non legato and legato and their combination; **staccato and pedal introduced**.
  - 14–18 works: 6–8 pieces (including some with polyphonic elements), 2–3 large-form works, 6–8 etudes.
  - Technical: scales up to 1 sharp or flat. Major scales HT, 2 octaves, plus contrary motion. Minor scales (all three forms) HS. Chromatic HT. Arpeggios HS. Chords HT.
- **Year 3:** 3–4 polyphonic pieces (imitative and sub-voice, the Russian "podgolosochnaya" folk-song style), 2–3 large-form works (sonatina movements, short variations), 4–6 pieces, 6–10 etudes.
  - Technical: majors to 1 sharp or flat, **4 octaves** in parallel and contrary motion. Harmonic minor in contrary motion. Chromatic 4 octaves. Short arpeggios HT, 4 octaves; long arpeggios 2 octaves. Tonic triads with inversions HT, 4 octaves.
  - Chord progressions: triads with inversions on I, IV and V.
  - Technical exam in the 3rd quarter: 2 scales out of 6 (C, A minor, G, E minor, F, D minor) at medium tempo.
  - Sight-reading: easy year-1 repertoire.
- **Year 4:** keys up to 2 sharps or flats, 4 octaves HT in parallel and contrary motion. Melodic minor in parallel motion. Short and long arpeggios HT, 4 octaves. Broken arpeggios HS.
  - D7: build and resolve.
  - Progressions: T–S–D–T and T–S–K6/4–D–T.
  - 2–3 polyphonic pieces, 2–3 large-form works, 4–6 pieces, 6–8 etudes (for example Czerny Op. 23, edited by Kalman).
  - Sight-reading at year-2 level.
- **Year 5:** keys up to 3 sharps or flats, 4 octaves HT. **Scales in 3rds and 10ths.** Chromatic contrary motion. Arpeggios at fast tempo. Broken arpeggios HT. D7 and diminished-7th arpeggios HS. 3–4-note chords. D7 and diminished-7th sequences.
  - Etudes for the left hand.
  - Technical exam at a "fairly fast" tempo.
  - Sight-reading at year 2–3 level; independent learning of a year-3 piece.
- **Year 6:** keys up to 4 sharps or flats; adds 3rds, 10ths and 6ths; D7 and diminished-7th arpeggios with inversions. Sight-reading at year-3 level; independent learning at year-4 level.
- **Years 7–8:** 12–14 works a year; scales up to 5 and then 6 sharps or flats, fast. Graduation exam: 5 works from memory (an etude, two pieces, polyphony, large form).
- **Assessment cycle:**
  - Etude competition at the end of the 1st quarter.
  - Technical exam in the 3rd quarter from year 3.
  - Semester tests.
  - End-of-year promotion exam (years 1–7).
- **Textbooks named:**
  - A. Nikolaev, "School of Piano Playing" (Muzgiz, 1960), plus Nikolaev etude collections.
  - B. Milich, "To the Little Pianist" (Kyiv, 1977), and Milich etude collections (year 2).
  - L. Barenboim and N. Perunova, "Path to Music" (Leningrad, 1988).
  - Czerny Op. 23 (ed. Kalman).
  - Artobolevskaya is not named in this document.
- **Hours** (federal-standard framework): classroom specialty lessons are 2 h/week in years 1–4 and 2.5 h/week in years 5–8. Home self-study norms are **3 h/week in years 1–2**, 4 h in 3–4, 5 h in 5–6 and 6 h in 7–8. Entry age is 6.5–9, and lessons last 40 minutes. — [Chastoozerskaya DShI programme framework](https://chshi.uralschool.ru/file/download?id=2296); [Verkhnie Sergi DShI programme](https://vsergi-dshi.uralschool.ru/file/download?id=1337)
- **Lithuania:** a hobby-level ("mėgėjų") music programme does not set per-year piano scales. Over the whole course it requires 1–2 polyphonic works, 1–2 large-form works, 2–4 pieces and 2–4 etudes plus technical exercises. Assessment is in a freely chosen form (open lesson, concert, competition). — [JMM hobby music programme annex](https://jmm.lt/storage/4600/Muzikos-megeju-ugdymo-programos-priedas.pdf)

### Inferences
- Soviet-tradition sequence for year 1: single-line pieces in non legato → legato → two-hand pieces. Staccato and pedal are deferred to year 2. This matches the brief's "non-legato before legato" hypothesis. Non legato means whole-arm, free-fall weight before finger legato.
- The fixed genre quadrant (etude / polyphony / large form / pieces) is a strong template for a "balanced diet" rule in a mastery tree: each level requires at least one item from each branch.
- Self-study norms of 3–6 h/week (~25–50 min/day) match the target learner's 20–40 min/day. The child pupil also gets 2 h/week of teacher contact plus solfeggio and music literature, which an app must substitute for.
- By year 5 a DMSh pupil is roughly at ABRSM G5–6 technically (4-octave scales, 3rds, D7 and diminished-7th arpeggios). The DMSh is technically ahead of ABRSM at the same year count, because of the 4-octave scales from year 3.

### Gaps
- Artobolevskaya's "First Meeting with Music" sequencing (the pre-notation period, singing and playing by ear first) was not confirmed from a fetched source.
- Russian scale tempo norms are qualitative only ("medium", "moderate", "fairly fast"). No bpm figures were found.
- Lithuanian pre-professional (formal) music-school piano programmes, as opposed to hobby programmes, were not found. Several Russian PDFs (edu.tatar.ru) failed to fetch.

## 4. Method books' first-year order; adult vs child methods

### Takeaway
Adult methods (Alfred's Adult All-in-One, Faber Adult Piano Adventures) move fast and are chord-first. Alfred covers C, G7 and F chords within the first ~35 pages, then G position, eighths, the sustain pedal, the C/G/F major and A/D minor scales, primary chords, the blues progression, syncopation and triplets, all in one book that is roughly year 1 for an adult. Faber's adult book stresses lead sheets and chord symbols alongside reading.

### Cited Findings
**Alfred's Adult All-in-One Level 1, in page order** — [Alfred's Adult All-in-One Level 1 (studylib copy)](https://studylib.net/doc/25598956/alfred-s-adult-all-in-one-piano-course)
- Posture, finger numbers, white-key names.
- RH C position and treble clef, then LH C position and bass clef.
- Quarter, half and whole notes; rests; grand staff; time signature; p, mf and f.
- Melodic and harmonic intervals 2nd–5th.
- **C major and G7 chords and chord symbols** (pp. 32+); B and A; ties and slurs (legato); dotted half note and 3/4; **F chord**; pickups.
- G position; sharp sign; G and D7 chords; block vs broken chords; **damper pedal**.
- Middle C position; cresc., dim. and fermata; **eighth notes**; D.C. al Fine; **dotted quarter**; Hanon.
- 6ths; staccato; 7ths and octaves; flat sign; half and whole steps; tetrachords.
- **C major scale**, primary chords and progressions, **blues progression**.
- G major scale; changing fingers on the same note; **syncopation**; G blues scale.
- F major scale; eighth rest; accent; new bass style; arpeggiated chords.
- **A harmonic minor**; overlapping (legato) pedal; triads.
- D harmonic minor.
- **Eighth-note triplets**; summary of the most-used keys and their primary chords.

**Faber Adult Piano Adventures Book 1** — [Faber Piano Adventures](https://pianoadventures.com/piano-books/adult-piano-adventures/level-1/things-to-know/)
- Features: a sequenced reading plan, classical, jazz, folk and spiritual repertoire, sightreading and ear-training, **lead sheets with chord symbols and LH chord patterns**, theory review pages, and a "3-minute technique" routine. Each unit introduces one new concept while reviewing earlier ones.
- Individual video units include "Unit 10: Sharps and Flats", "Unit 12: The C Major Scale" and "Unit 13: The G7 Chord". — [Piano Adventures videos](https://pianoadventures.com/blog/videos/unit-13-the-g7-chord-adult-piano-adventures-book-1/)

### Inferences
- An adult's first year (≈ one adult method book) reaches roughly ABRSM Initial–Grade 1 reading, with Grade 2-ish harmony knowledge: primary chords in 5 keys, the blues progression and lead-sheet basics. Adult methods trade fewer reading positions for earlier chords and broader styles.
- Alfred introduces the damper pedal in the first book, with overlapping pedal by the A minor unit. The Russian DMSh defers pedal to year 2, and ABRSM sight-reading does not use it until G6.
- The concept order (C position → chords → G position → eighths → intervals to the octave → scales and primary chords → minor) is a sound skeleton for an app's year 1.

### Gaps
- Tables of contents for Faber Piano Adventures (child Primer–Level 5), Bastien, John Thompson and Suzuki Book 1 were not fetched. From general knowledge, **not verified here**:
  - Faber uses an "intervallic plus landmark" reading approach starting off-staff.
  - Thompson is middle-C-oriented.
  - Bastien is multi-key (all keys in 5-finger positions early).
  - Suzuki is by ear and rote first, starting with "Twinkle" variations, with reading delayed.

## 5. Time to reach each grade; what "high intermediate" means; is 5 years realistic

### Takeaway
On ABRSM's own hours, an adult at 30–40 min/day most days would reach roughly Grade 5 in 3–5 years and Grade 6 in 4–6 years. "High intermediate" (ABRSM G6–7 / RCM L6–7) after 5 years is plausible only at the upper end of the practice range and with efficient, teacher-like guidance. Grade 5–6 is the realistic 5-year target.

### Cited Findings
- ABRSM total qualification time sums to about 640 h for Initial to G5 and about 860 h to G6 (see table in section 1). — [ABRSM 2025 & 2026 spec](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf)
- A London teacher's blog gives these "orientative" timelines: Grade 1 in 6–12 months; Grade 5 at about 4 years (children 4–5 years, adults 2.5–4 years); Grade 8 in 8–10 years (adults 5–8 years). Grades 4–5 go with 30–45 min/day of practice. A sample adult plan reaches Grade 5 in 3 years with weekly 45-minute lessons and practice rising from 30 to 60 min/day. This is a lower-authority source and is opinion. — [piano-composer-teacher-london.co.uk](https://www.piano-composer-teacher-london.co.uk/how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-piano/)
- The Russian DMSh (a children's model) reaches 4-octave scales with 3rds and 7th-chord arpeggios, roughly G5–6 technique, in year 5–6 with 3–5 h/week of home practice plus lessons. — [Rachmaninov school programme](https://rahmaninovschool.spb.ru/f/dpop_2020_8_fortepiano_spec_i_chtenie.pdf); [Chastoozerskaya framework](https://chshi.uralschool.ru/file/download?id=2296)

### Inferences
- **Proposed 5-year benchmark map** (synthesised; tempos from ABRSM and RCM):
  - **Year 1:** ABRSM Initial–G1 / RCM Prep A–L1. 5-finger positions → 1-octave scales HS (q≈60, eighths). C, G and F triads and G7. 4/4 and 3/4. Quarter to dotted-quarter values. Sight-reading of 4 bars, HS, 5-finger. Ear: major/minor triads, 4-note playback, 2-bar clapback.
  - **Year 2:** G2 / RCM L2. 2-octave HT scales in keys up to 1–2 sharps or flats (q≈66–80). First chromatic scale. Triads with inversions. Hands-together sight-reading. Ties. Staccato. Pedal introduced. Ear: m3, M3 and P5 intervals.
  - **Year 3:** G3–4 / RCM L3–4. Scales up to 3–4 sharps or flats HT at q=80–100. First arpeggios. 3/8 and 6/8. Anacrusis. Semiquavers. Reading outside 5-finger position with 2-note chords. Ear: P4 and chord members.
  - **Year 4:** G5 / RCM L5. All keys. Chromatic HT. D7 and diminished 7th. I–V–I cadences. Syncopation. Theory ≈ ABRSM G5 (the prerequisite for G6). Scales h=60.
  - **Year 5:** G6 / RCM L6. 4-octave HT scales at h=72 (ABRSM) or 2-octave scales in sixteenths at q=60–76 (RCM). Dominant and diminished 7th arpeggios. Triplets. 5/8, 5/4 and 9/8. Pedalling in sight-reading. Repertoire across the four style periods.
- "High intermediate" in the owner's sense ≈ ABRSM G6–7 / RCM L6–7. For an adult at 20–40 min/day this is ambitious. G5–6 by year 5 is the honest target, with G7 as a stretch goal for learners who practise toward the top of the range.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or exam-board data on actual adult progression rates was found. The blog figures are anecdotal.
- RCM publishes no official hours per level that could be compared with ABRSM's.
