## A second staff for low notes

The treble staff covers about an octave and a half above middle C. That is plenty for a tune, but the left hand spends most of its time lower down, and writing those low notes on the treble staff would need a forest of ledger lines below it. So low notes get a staff of their own, marked with a different clef: the **bass clef** (say "base", as in the bottom of something).

## The F clef

The bass clef looks like a backwards C with two dots after it. Like the treble clef, it grew from a letter, in this case a handwritten **F**. Its two dots sit on either side of the **fourth line from the bottom**, and that line is **F**, the F just below middle C (F3). That is why it is also called the **F clef**.

```figure
staff: { notes: "F3:w", clef: bass, labels: names }
caption: The two dots of the bass clef hug the F line, the fourth line up.
```

Everything else works exactly as on the treble staff. Each step from a line to the next space is one letter, one white key. The space above the F line is G, the top line is A. The line below F is D.

```figure
staff: { notes: "G2:q A2:q B2:q C3:q D3:q E3:q F3:q G3:q A3:q", clef: bass, labels: names }
caption: The bass staff from its bottom line, G2, to its top line, A3.
```

> **The bass clef** has two dots round the F line (fourth line from the bottom). It is used for the left hand and lower notes.

## Middle C from below

Middle C sits on a ledger line just **above** the bass staff, the mirror image of where it sits on the treble staff. Just below it, hanging from the top line, is B3, and the top line itself is A3.

```figure
staff: { notes: "C4:q B3:q A3:h", clef: bass, labels: names }
keys: { notes: "A3 B3 C4", labels: names }
caption: Middle C on a ledger line above the bass staff, then B3 and A3 on the top line.
```

So middle C really is in the middle: one ledger line above the bass staff and one ledger line below the treble staff. Same key, same note, written two ways depending on which staff it belongs to.

## Landmarks in the bass

The most useful bass landmarks are:

- **Middle C** (C4) on its ledger line above the staff.
- **F3** on the fourth line, where the dots are.
- **C3**, the C an octave below middle C, in the second space.
- **G2** on the bottom line.

```figure
staff: { notes: "G2:q C3:q F3:q C4:q", clef: bass, labels: names }
caption: Four bass landmarks: bottom-line G, second-space C, F between the dots, middle C.
```

## Memory aids, and a warning

The bass lines, bottom to top, are **G B D F A**: "**G**ood **B**oys **D**eserve **F**ootball **A**lways". The spaces are **A C E G**: "**A**ll **C**ows **E**at **G**rass".

```figure
staff: { notes: "A2:q C3:q E3:q G3:q", clef: bass, labels: names }
caption: The bass spaces: All Cows Eat Grass.
check:
  ask: "A note sits on the top line of the bass staff. Which note is it?"
  choices: ["A", "F", "G"]
  correct: 0
  why: "The bass lines are **G B D F A** from the bottom, so the top line is **A** (A3). Above it come B3 and then middle C on its ledger line."
```

Here is the warning. The bass staff is **not** the treble staff with the same names. Every line and space has a different letter. The bottom line is E in the treble but G in the bass. Many pianists find the bass clef harder for years because they learned the treble first and keep "reading treble" by mistake. The cure is to treat it as its own language from the start, with its own landmarks, and to read a little bass every day. The reading drills in this app mix both clefs for exactly this reason.

A handy link, if you want one: every bass note is two letters "up" from the treble note in the same place. Bottom line: E in treble, G in bass. But do not rely on translating. It adds a step in your head that you will want to lose.

## Why two clefs and not one?

You might wonder why we do not simply use one very tall staff. The answer is the eye. Five lines are about as many as anyone can take in at a glance; with eleven, every note would need counting. Splitting the range into two five-line staves, with middle C between them, keeps each one readable. In the next chapter you will see how they join into the **grand staff** that all piano music uses.

## Common confusions

- **Reading bass as treble.** The most common error of all. Check the clef before every piece.
- **Where the dots are.** The dots are around the F line, not on it. The line between them is F.
- **Middle C on the bass staff.** It is on a ledger line *above* the bass staff, not below.

## In short

The bass clef, or F clef, puts its two dots round the fourth line, F3. It is used for the left hand and lower notes. Middle C sits on a ledger line above it. The lines are G B D F A, the spaces A C E G, and every name is different from the treble staff, so learn the bass landmarks as their own set.
