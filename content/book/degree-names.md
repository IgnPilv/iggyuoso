## Numbers and names

You have been calling the notes of a scale by number: 1, 2, 3 up to 7. Musicians also have a set of traditional **names** for the scale degrees. They are worth learning because the names describe what each note does, and because books, teachers and fellow musicians use them all the time ("the melody ends on the mediant", "a chord on the dominant").

| Degree | Name | In C major | Its character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **tonic** | C | home, rest |
| 2 | **supertonic** | D | just above the tonic, restless |
| 3 | **mediant** | E | halfway between tonic and dominant, stable and colourful |
| 4 | **subdominant** | F | a gentle step away, leans down to 3 |
| 5 | **dominant** | G | the strongest note after the tonic, a pillar |
| 6 | **submediant** | A | halfway down between tonic and subdominant |
| 7 | **leading note** | B | a semitone below the tonic, pulls up hard |

## Where the names come from

The names are built around two pillars, the **tonic** (from "tone", the main note) and the **dominant** (the "ruling" note, because after the tonic it is the most important). Everything else is named by its position relative to those two:

- **Supertonic**: "super" means above. It is the note just above the tonic.
- **Mediant**: "in the middle". It sits halfway between the tonic and the dominant (C, E, G: E is in the middle).
- **Subdominant**: "under the dominant". It is the note just below the dominant, but its name also has a second meaning: it is a **5th below the tonic**, just as the dominant is a 5th above. C up a 5th is G (dominant); C down a 5th is F (subdominant).
- **Submediant**: "under the mediant". It is halfway between the tonic and the subdominant going *down* (C, A, F: A is in the middle), a mirror image of the mediant.
- **Leading note**: it leads to the tonic.

> **Tonic** is home; the **dominant** (5th above) and **subdominant** (5th below) are its two pillars; the **mediant** and **submediant** sit halfway towards them; the **supertonic** is just above home and the **leading note** just below.

## In minor keys

The names are the same in minor, with one detail. In the natural minor, the 7th degree is a whole tone below the tonic and is called the **subtonic** (A minor: G). When it is raised to a semitone below the tonic, as in the harmonic and melodic minor (G♯), it is the **leading note** again. So "leading note" always means the note a semitone below the tonic.

## Names for chords too

The same names are used for the chords built on each degree. The **tonic chord** is the triad on 1 (I), the **dominant chord** the triad on 5 (V), the **subdominant chord** the triad on 4 (IV). So the three "primary chords" you met earlier are the tonic, subdominant and dominant chords. When you read that a piece "moves to the dominant", it means it moves towards the chord, or key, built on the 5th degree.

## Hearing the degrees

Each degree has the same feeling in every key, and recognising degrees by ear is one of the most powerful listening skills there is. It is how people pick out tunes on the piano by ear. The ear branch trains it like this: a short chord progression sets up the key, then one note sounds, and you find it. Some tips:

- The **tonic** sounds finished.
- The **dominant** sounds strong and open but not finished, like a comma.
- The **mediant** sounds sweet and stable, the colour of the key (major or minor).
- The **leading note** sounds like it is about to step up.
- The **subdominant** sounds suspended, wanting to fall to the mediant.
- The **supertonic** and **submediant** sound like passing places, wanting to move.

## Common confusions

- **Subdominant means "below the tonic".** It is the 4th degree: a 5th below the tonic, or a step below the dominant.
- **Mediant and submediant are neighbours.** They are not: one is degree 3, the other degree 6, mirror images round the tonic.
- **Leading note in natural minor.** In natural minor the 7th is a tone below and is the subtonic.

## In short

The scale degrees are tonic, supertonic, mediant, subdominant, dominant, submediant and leading note. The names come from two pillars, the tonic and the dominant a 5th above, with the subdominant a 5th below and the mediants halfway. In natural minor the 7th is the subtonic. The same names are used for chords, and each degree has a feeling you can learn to hear.
