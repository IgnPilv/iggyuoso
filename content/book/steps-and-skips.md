## Reading like you read words

When you read this sentence you do not spell out each word letter by letter. You see shapes and take them in whole. Music reading can work the same way. Beginners name every note ("that's E, that's G, that's F..."), which is slow and tiring. Experienced readers name the first note and then mostly read **how far each note is from the one before**, and whether it goes up or down. The hand already knows what "one key up" and "two keys down" feel like, so it simply follows the picture.

The distance between two notes has a name: an **interval**. You will learn many intervals in later chapters. This chapter is about the two smallest, which make up most of the notes in simple tunes: the **step** and the **skip**.

## The step

A **step** is a move to the very next letter: C to D, G to F, B to C. On the keyboard it is the next white key, up or down. On the staff it is the easiest shape there is:

- From a **line** to the **space** right next to it, or
- From a **space** to the **line** right next to it.

The notes almost touch. In the language of intervals, a step is a **2nd**: count the letters, starting from the first one as 1. C (1), D (2): a 2nd.

```figure

caption: Steps up and down. Line, space, line, space: the notes look like stairs.
```

Tunes that move mostly by step are smooth and easy to sing. Think of the start of *Ode to Joy*: E E F G G F E D. Almost every move is a step.

## The skip

A **skip** jumps over one letter: C to E (skipping D), G to B, F to D going down. On the keyboard you skip over one white key. On the staff the shape is just as clear:

- From a **line** to the **next line**, or
- From a **space** to the **next space**.

A skip is a **3rd**: C (1), D (2), E (3).

```figure
staff: { notes: "C4:q E4:q G4:q E4:q | D4:q F4:q A4:q F4:q", labels: names }
sound: "C4:q E4:q G4:q E4:q D4:q F4:q A4:q F4:q"
caption: Skips. Line to line (C E G) or space to space (D F A). One note is left out each time.
```

> **Step = 2nd**: line to space or space to line, the next key. **Skip = 3rd**: line to line or space to space, jumping over one key. Count both ends when you count intervals.

## Why count both ends?

This trips everyone up the first time. From C to E, there are two moves (C to D, D to E), so why is it called a 3rd? Because intervals are named by how many letters they cover, counting both the note you start on and the note you land on: C, D, E is three letters. It is the same way some languages say "in eight days" for a week: Monday to Monday, counting both Mondays. It feels odd for a day and then becomes automatic. It also means the smallest move, to the next key, is a 2nd, and staying on the same note is a **unison** (or "1st").

## Reading a tune by its shape

Here is the method in practice. Look at the first note and find it from a landmark. From then on, for each new note ask two questions: **up or down?** and **step or skip (or bigger)?** Then let your finger move.

```figure
staff: { notes: "E4:q D4:q C4:q D4:q | E4:q E4:q E4:h | D4:q D4:q D4:h | E4:q G4:q G4:h", meter: 4/4, labels: names }
sound: "E4:q D4:q C4:q D4:q E4:q E4:q E4:h D4:q D4:q D4:h E4:q G4:q G4:h"
caption: "Mary Had a Little Lamb". Steps down, steps up, repeated notes, and one skip up at the end.
```

Read this one by shape: start on E (the bottom line), step down, step down, step up, step up, same, same; step down, same, same; step up, skip up, same. You only had to name one note.

In a five-finger position this is even easier, because steps and skips match your fingers: a step is the next finger, a skip jumps over one finger. Thumb to middle finger is always a skip.

## Repeated notes, and bigger leaps

Two more shapes complete the beginner's toolkit:

- A **repeat**: the same note again, sitting on the same line or space. Nothing moves.
- A **leap**: anything bigger than a skip. From a line to the line two above is a 5th (C to G, say). You will learn to read these by eye in the chapter on intervals.

## Common confusions

- **A 3rd is two keys away, not three.** Count the letters, both ends included: C D E.
- **Line to line is always a skip**, even though the two notes do not look far apart. Line to space is always a step.
- **Ignoring direction.** "Step" says how far; you still have to see whether it goes up or down.

## In short

An interval is the distance between two notes, named by counting letters from the first to the last, both included. A step (2nd) goes line to space or space to line; a skip (3rd) goes line to line or space to space. Name the first note, then read the rest by direction and distance, and let your hand follow the shape.
