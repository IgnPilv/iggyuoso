## Why write music down at all?

For most of history, music was learned by ear: someone played or sang it, and you copied. That works, but it has limits. Tunes change as they pass from person to person, and long or complicated pieces are hard to remember. About a thousand years ago, monks in Europe who had to learn hundreds of chants started drawing lines to show how high or low each note should be. An Italian monk, Guido of Arezzo, is usually credited with the idea of a set of lines where each line and each space stands for one fixed note. He claimed his singers could learn in days what used to take years. The system has grown since, but the heart of it is still his.

## The staff

Music is written on a **staff** (British books say **stave**): five horizontal lines, with four spaces between them.

```figure
staff: { notes: "E4:q F4:q G4:q A4:q B4:q C5:q D5:q E5:q F5:q", labels: names }
caption: The treble staff from its bottom line, E, to its top line, F. Line, space, line, space...
```

Each note is drawn as an oval. A note can sit in one of two ways:

- **On a line**: the line runs straight through the middle of the oval.
- **In a space**: the oval sits between two lines, touching both.

Lines and spaces are counted **from the bottom up**: the bottom line is the first line, the space above it is the first space, and so on, up to the fifth (top) line.

## Higher on the page is higher in sound

The staff is a picture of pitch, just like the keyboard. The higher a note sits on the staff, the higher it sounds. And each step up the staff, from a line to the space just above, or from a space to the line just above, is the **next letter of the alphabet**, the next white key to the right.

```figure
staff: { notes: "C4:q D4:q E4:q F4:q G4:q", labels: names }
keys: { notes: "C4 D4 E4 F4 G4", labels: names }
sound: "C4:q D4:q E4:q F4:q G4:q"
caption: Each step up on the staff is one white key to the right.
```

That is all the staff is: a ladder where every rung, line or space, is one white key. If you know the name of one rung, you can count up or down to any other.

## The clef tells you where you are

A ladder is no use unless you know which rung is which. That is the job of the **clef**, the large sign at the very start of each staff. The word comes from the French for "key": it unlocks the names of the lines.

The clef you will meet first is the **treble clef**. It is used for higher notes, most of the time for the **right hand**. Its curly shape grew, over centuries of handwriting, out of a fancy letter G, and its inner curl wraps around the second line from the bottom. That line is **G**, the G just above middle C (G4). This is why the treble clef is also called the **G clef**.

```figure
staff: { notes: "G4:w", labels: names }
caption: The treble clef curls round the second line. That line is G4.
```

Once you know that the second line is G, everything else follows: the space above is A, the next line B, and so on up; the space below is F, the bottom line E.

> **The treble clef** curls round the G line (second line from the bottom). It is used for the right hand and higher notes. Find G, then step up or down one letter at a time.

## Middle C and its neighbours

Middle C is too low to fit on the treble staff. It sits just below it, on a short line of its own called a **ledger line**. Ledger lines are little extra lines that extend the staff upwards or downwards, one for each note that needs it.

```figure
staff: { notes: "C4:q D4:q E4:h", labels: names }
keys: { notes: "C4 D4 E4", labels: names }
caption: Middle C on a ledger line, D hanging under the bottom line, E on the bottom line.
```

- **Middle C (C4)**: on a ledger line below the staff.
- **D4**: just below the bottom line, hanging from it.
- **E4**: on the bottom line.

These three, together with the G line, are your first **landmark notes**: notes you know instantly, without counting, and use as starting points.

## Memory aids

Generations of piano pupils have learned the treble staff with little sentences. The lines, bottom to top, are **E G B D F**: "**E**very **G**ood **B**oy **D**eserves **F**ootball" (or fudge, or fruit). The spaces, bottom to top, spell **F A C E**, a word in itself.

```figure
staff: { notes: "E4:q G4:q B4:q D5:q F5:q", labels: names }
caption: "Every Good Boy Deserves Football": the treble lines.
```

```figure
staff: { notes: "F4:q A4:q C5:q E5:q", labels: names }
caption: The spaces spell FACE.
check:
  ask: "Which note sits in the second space of the treble staff?"
  choices: ["A", "G", "C"]
  correct: 0
  why: "The spaces spell **F A C E** from the bottom: first space F, second space **A**."
```

These sentences are useful for the first week. But be careful: if you have to recite a whole sentence for every note, reading will stay slow. The aim is to recognise notes on sight, the way you recognise letters in words. Landmarks help more in the long run: know middle C, E on the bottom line, G on the second line, and the C in the third space (C5) by heart, and reach every other note by counting a step or two from the nearest landmark.

## Reading by eye and by hand

Reading music has two halves: knowing what the note is called, and finding it on the keyboard. Good readers skip the name altogether. They see a note on the second line and their finger goes to G4 without saying "G" in their head. The reading drills in this app train exactly this. They show a note and ask you to play it, and the more you do them the less you will need to think.

## Common confusions

- **On a line or in a space?** If the line goes through the middle of the oval, it is on the line. If the oval sits between two lines, it is in a space. Look closely at the edges.
- **Counting lines from the top.** Always count from the bottom.
- **Middle C is not on the staff.** It needs its own ledger line, below the treble staff and above the bass staff.

## In short

Music is written on a staff of five lines and four spaces; higher on the staff means higher in sound, and each step from line to space is the next letter. The treble clef curls round the G line and is used for the right hand. Middle C sits on a ledger line below the treble staff. Learn the landmarks, middle C, bottom-line E, second-line G and third-space C, and count from them.
